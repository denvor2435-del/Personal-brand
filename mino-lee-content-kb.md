## D0: How to watch this (0:00)
- Mino's story: on 1 Jan 2024, a broke 20-year-old student, he posted one reel a day. He says he doubled to 100K followers in 60 days and got a business to $10K/month in 15 days.
- Bookmark the course and do one module a day for 7 days.
- **"Content creation must become a habit before it becomes a skill and before it becomes an income."** (1:30)

## D1: Day 1, your first video (2:06)
**Goal:** get one video posted. Views don't matter this week. "This is where 90% of people fall off." (4:34)

**The 4 questions** (one honest sentence each, "don't even think about content"):
1. Hi, my name is ___. I'm ___ years old and I'm currently a ___ (simple, surface-level label/occupation).
2. Why am I starting content creation?
3. What is my biggest fear with content creation?
4. A message to someone with the same fear: encouragement ("you're not alone") or a call to action ("join me").

**Script template** (copy your answers in):
> "Today is day one of posting every day for 7 days as a [age]-year-old [label]. I'm starting this content challenge because [Q2]. Personally, [Q3]. So if you have that same [fear], [Q4]. I'll see you guys tomorrow for day two."

The hook gives people "a reason for actually getting to know you" instead of "Hi, my name is…" (8:07)

**Extra task:** message one other creator in the community and connect. "It's a lot easier when you have somebody else in your corner." (6:14)

**Filming (CapCut):**
- Script in Google Docs, then copy it into CapCut's camera teleprompter. Set recording to 3 minutes. (9:06)
- **Front lighting:** face the brightest light source (a window). Never have the light behind you. (10:40)
- Prop the phone up (he uses a water bottle).
- Calm your nerves: breathe in, hold 6s, out 8s, then take a big breath right before you record. (11:32)
- If you mess up, just record that sentence again.

**Editing (CapCut):**
- Cut the dead space at the start, and between sentences, down to the frame. (14:24)
- Auto captions with default settings. Fix typos. **Never two sentences on one line.** (18:13; 19:40)
- Make captions slightly smaller and move them ~30% up from the bottom, clear of the username/caption area and the side buttons. (21:41)
- Voice volume 200%. Export 4K, 30 fps. (22:20; 22:54)

**Posting:** upload the exported file in the app. Short caption, plus **one niche hashtag** for new accounts. (24:28)

## D2: Day 2, personal story video (25:31)
**Mindset:** forget about "making content" and don't copy other creators. Start from your own life, because "events on their own are not boring. It's how we spin them." (28:06)

**Questions:**
1. List the 3 most interesting things that happened in the last 7 days (pick the ones with the most emotion). One sentence each.
2. The strongest emotion you felt in each (1–2 words).
3. What made the moment special enough to make you feel that? (2 sentences: what was new, what details stood out)
4. A life lesson you learned, plus a prior belief that changed ("Before I thought…").

**Script template (Mad Lib):**
> "[Recently / in the past week / yesterday], [event, Q1]. [What made it special, Q3]. And this was honestly so [emotion, Q2] because in that moment I realized [lesson, Q4]. [Before I thought …]." + a positive call to action or affirmation.

**Revision rules:**
- The **first 3 sentences matter most**. Never say "hey guys" or "welcome back"; you're talking to strangers. (33:10)
- Make each sentence flow into the next. Cut redundant words.
- End on a positive note so people feel good and come back. (34:51)
- Aim each video at whoever relates most, e.g. "fellow creators", "fellow business owners". (35:07)

**Filming:** record 1–2 sentences per take so you don't run out of breath. Chest up. (37:36) Stand close to your main light, and put colour in the background. (36:43)

**New editing skills:** B-roll overlay (muted, loosely related to what you're saying; "the B-roll doesn't really have to completely correlate"). (44:25) Thumbnail trick: add "Day N" text, screenshot it, crop it, and set it as the cover. (46:28) You can hide reels from the profile grid. (48:01)

**Expectations:** "a proper expectation in the first 30 days [is] to get your first video over 10K." Results are exponential. It took him 3 years to hit his first $10K month from content. (49:58)

**Extra task:** review another member's video and leave encouragement. (51:02)

## P1: Pro tip, mindset (52:03)
- His whole strategy is **talking-head videos about your life, experiences and knowledge**. Personal branding works when you're "so embarrassingly… terrifyingly yourself" that the right people find you. (53:02)
- **Three fears:** fear of being seen, imposter syndrome, lack of faith and discipline. (53:32)
- **"If you feel you're not qualified, good."** If you're on step two, you're the best person to help the people stuck on step one. (54:26)
- **You only need 1,000 people** who truly get you. (55:14)
- In short-form you're not their whole guide. You get them to **take one step**. (56:03)
- **3 systems to keep the fire going:**
  1. Write down 6-month goals (followers, income, **posts per day**) and put them where you'll see them every day. (57:57)
  2. **Daily action:** post once a day and only congratulate yourself for showing up. (59:42)
  3. **Lower your view expectations:** 1,000 views is two lecture halls of people. (1:00:19)
- Business maths: 10K views × 1% into your funnel × 10% buy at $100 = $1,000. "You don't need millions of views." (1:01:03)
- "The science of audience building is being your authentic self." (1:02:00)

## D3: Day 3, message to your younger self (1:02:29)
"Almost all my videos are messages to my past self because that's who I can help the most." (1:04:32) He says it was his best-performing format.

**Prep:** 5–10 B-roll clips.

**Questions** (think of yourself exactly one year ago; close your eyes for 20 seconds):
1. List 5 specific things that happened this year your younger self would be hyped about.
2. One **specific** change it took for each (not "work hard": something that would surprise your younger self). (1:07:53)
3. **The doubt:** what would your younger self say back, in their words? *"One of the biggest hacks that I have for content scripting."* (1:10:08)
4. Your reply to that doubt: 3–4 sentences, detailed, with specific numbers.
5. A final short message: inspire, reassure or suggest one action.

**Script structure:** hook (a very specific outcome) → the change that got you there → "Now, I know you're probably wondering/thinking [doubt]" → convince them → encouragement.
> "The key with a short form video is not to walk them A through Z… [it's] to convince them, hey, think this way and just change your mind about this little belief." (1:17:37)

**Hook editing:** cut every redundant word from the first sentences and make it more specific (e.g. "my **Korean** mom"). (1:14:15)

**Filming:** warm up (tongue twisters), use your hands, and picture the camera as your younger self. (1:19:10)

**Editing:**
- Change the B-roll every ~5 seconds or every sentence or half-sentence. Start each clip on movement, not static frames. (1:22:49; 1:26:06)
- Random B-roll is fine: "the randomness too just gives it a very authentic feel." (1:28:05)
- Background music: a familiar song without loud lyrics, ~60–70% volume. Trim the audio at the end of the video. (1:29:21; 1:32:00)

**Afterwards:** post the link in the community for review and review someone else's. (1:34:46)

## P2: Pro tip, B-roll (1:36:29)
He doesn't plan B-roll and has no shot list. He has 3 go-to shots he captures through the day:
1. **0.5x shots:** phone propped up while you work, journal, read or train; also handheld 0.5x while walking.
2. **1x shot of your laptop screen:** for numbers, revenue, Discord or anything else on screen. Film the laptop rather than using a screenshot, because it "looks so much more cinematic". (1:37:57)
3. **Time-lapses:** eating, morning routine, working (usually 0.5x, 10–30 min).

Length: 10 seconds for laptop and walking shots; 30 seconds+ for working, gym and reading, so you have a usable stretch. (1:39:30)

## D4: Day 4, building a brand around your life (1:40:26)
**Three tasks:** establish your life brand's purpose and identity, optimise your profiles, and make your first life-brand video.

**Examples he shows:** his own "Road to $100K/month", a friend vlogging until the end of the semester, someone writing a film in 30 days, a road to a first bodybuilding show, a road to $1M by end of 2024, a road to $100K revenue for a startup. (1:42:27)

**Questions:**
1. **How do you want to help people with your brand?** What outcome do viewers get? This is what you come back to when the views are bad. (1:43:24)
2. **Pick a goal that is specific and binary.** There's a day you hit it and every day before you haven't. "Become a better X" doesn't work. People "live vicariously through" a clear storyline. (1:44:51)
   - You can switch the goal later. "It's more so essentially a marketing tool." (1:47:39)
   - **"That's pretty much like my hack to getting views."** (1:46:30)
3. Tell a personal story about why this goal (4–5 sentences). It can be simple, even "I'm taking a course and Mino told me to choose a goal." (1:48:44)
4. Why are you pursuing it? Why does it matter now? (5–6 sentences)

**Script template:**
> "Today is day [N] of the road to [goal]. Now, why [goal]? Well, [Q3 story]. So I'm pursuing this goal because [Q4]. Follow to stay tuned on the journey, and I'll see you guys tomorrow. Peace."

End with something positive. "It's like a friend greeting you goodbye." (1:51:02)

**Profile optimisation:**
- **Bio line 1:** location and heritage (optionally age). It makes you feel like a real person. (1:51:45)
- **Bio line 2:** a life motto or tagline about *you* (or what you do / where you study).
- **Bio line 3:** the goal, e.g. "Documenting every day till 100K a month". Keep it short. (1:52:43)
- **Bio line 4 (optional):** a call to action plus a link to your offer.
- **Profile picture:** a clear, close-up, shoulder-height headshot. It's tiny on screen. (1:53:28)
- **Username:** no numbers at the end, because it looks unprofessional. (1:54:08)
- TikTok: the website link needs ~1,000 followers (he believes), so link Instagram until then. (1:55:13)

**Editing: hyper-cut opening.** In the first 3–4 seconds, cut B-roll clips under 1 second each, including a "stutter" (two parts of the same clip back to back) and sped-up clips. It hits the viewer with visual stimulus without stickers or clutter. After that, B-roll every 3–5 seconds, and never let a clip go static. (1:58:26–2:03:08)

## P3: Pro tip, editing (2:04:29)
**Fine clip cutting** is "probably the biggest hack… in editing… more important than making fancy captions." Zoom into the timeline to frame level (30 fps), cut to the last letter of each word, and remove every gap. It may only shave 3–4 seconds off a whole video, but the pacing feels far better. (2:04:29–2:07:43)

## D5: Day 5, unscripted video / minimum viable vlog (2:07:50)
Use it for busy days. Unscripted videos feel more natural and take less prep, but still need structure.

**Two questions:**
1. What events happened today?
2. What did I learn or realise from them, even something small? Pick what your younger self would value most.

**Hook writing:**
- Nobody cares about the bare event ("I ate lunch with my parents… Nobody asked"). (2:11:44)
- **Variation 1: rewrite the lesson so it's unique, contradictory and specific, not cliché.** "Today I learned I need to work harder" is cliché. (2:12:28)
  - Broad: "It's okay to feel out of place in certain environments." → more specific: "…in college." → most specific: "Just because you feel demotivated in college doesn't mean you're a lazy bum." (2:15:20–2:16:22)
  - "When I think of my hooks… I think about how can I make this really hit deeply to one person." (2:15:04)
  - Specific numbers work: "people eat up numbers" (e.g. "10K a month"). (2:18:02)
  - "When you try to pander to everybody on the internet, you will never reach anybody with depth." (2:18:27)
- **Variation 2: the event as a novel discovery.** Lead with the emotion and what's new: "After 20 years of my life, I finally feel like I'm getting over my fear of my parents' judgment." Skip quickly past boring events. (2:19:01)

**Telling the story:** use **"you" and "we"**: "You know that feeling when…". Picture telling it to your younger self or a friend, and assume viewers relate, because there are enough of them out there. (2:19:29–2:21:56)

**Finish:** a practical takeaway for the viewer, then "I'll see you guys tomorrow. Peace."

**Editing:** CapCut **transcript-based editing** (Pro, ~$8/month in his video) deletes fluffs and repeats from the transcript, then fine-trim. (2:23:56) Optional key-frame zoom on B-roll.

## D6: Day 6, optimising for virality (older module) (2:26:37)
He says it's an older TikTok-era module, but the principles still apply to Reels and TikTok.

**1. Niche + format.** Picking a **format** matters more than a niche: it's better for the algorithm, people know what to expect so they follow, and brainstorming gets faster. Exercise: write down 3 niches and 3 formats. If you're stuck, write down the format of the first 3 videos on your feed. (2:27:27)

**2. "Creativity on demand":** set a 10-minute timer and write 10 ideas (spend ~4 minutes listing concepts in your niche first). Expect only a few to be good. (2:30:20)

**3. Two things every viral video needs:** (2:33:50)
- **A hook that is clear** (the biggest pitfall is an unclear hook) **and quick** (no "hey guys").
- **Value** (entertainment or learning): assume the viewer finds you uninteresting by default. Spin overused tropes so they're new, e.g. "productive college day in the life *of a lazy student*". (2:37:51)

**4. Informational video template:** (2:39:31)
1. A one-second hook ("Here's how to fall asleep quicker.")
2. The solution straight away ("Stop using your phone an hour before bed.")
3. A common objection, for relatability ("Now I know you don't want to hear this…")
4. The meat: 60–70% of the video
5. Tie back to the hook at the end.

Draft it, then **read it out loud** and revise for concision and speed (two or more rounds). (2:40:46)

**5. Visuals = clarity + speed.** (2:46:13)
- Lighting: high contrast, simple.
- Framing: nothing important under the buttons or caption areas.
- Pacing: even half a second of dead air hurts. Something should change on screen at least every 3 seconds.

**6. Captions:** max ~4 words, one line, a familiar font (TikTok's default is Proxima Nova Semibold), in the middle or about 25% up. Never in the button or caption zones. (3:06:28–3:10:17)

## T: "4 tactics" growth video
Mino's claimed results: 259 videos in 12 months, 50K → 250K Instagram followers, $260K from his business. Examples of students using CapCut on iPhone: 400 → 60K, 2K → 60K followers.

### T1: Master the hook (1:24)
- The hook is the **first 5 seconds**. "I literally put 80% of my time that I spend scripting, planning, filming on just those first 5 seconds." (1:32)
- The algorithm mostly judges **viewer retention** (the % who watch to the end). (2:04)
- His example: a video at 298K views vs one at 5K views. A ~27% better hook at the start gave 54× the views. (2:16–2:52)
- "If your videos aren't hitting consistently above 10,000 views, you do not understand hooks to the degree you need to." (1:48)
- **AI for hooks:** he trained his own chatbot on hooks from his and his favourite creators' viral videos, so it sounds human. Generic GPT hooks "sound like absolute robots". (3:09) His bot is a free link in that video's description.
- **Delivery matters as much as wording:** "It's not about what you say… It's a lot more so about how you say it." (4:19) His 298K hook was **calm, "like I was on a FaceTime call with you"**, not loud. (5:03)
  - 298K hook (example): "You know, I really thought life would get better once I made 10K a month…" (4:55)

### T2: Know your ideal viewer persona (5:24)
- Most gurus focus on editing hacks. He focuses on **who he's talking to**. (5:31)
- "Viewer retention… comes down to simply how you make your viewers **feel**… not what you make your viewers think." (6:10)
- The strongest reason content gets shared: it **evokes a strong emotional response**. But don't act loud or angry; that creates an "uncanny valley" effect. Build a **genuine connection**, "like you're talking to an actual homie". (6:32–7:21)
- **Pick one real person you actually know** as your ideal viewer persona. Know them inside out: their life problems, what they wear, what they believe, what content they already watch. (7:28–8:00)
- **List their objections and answer them in the script.** His examples: "I don't have anything to sell" / "I'm busy" / "I'm too lazy to edit". (8:56–9:43)

### T3: Filming flow state (9:50)
- **Pre-filming routine:** deep breath, close your eyes, picture your persona right in front of you. Still stiff? Stand up and **shake it out** (from acting class). (9:58–10:43)
- **Hit record and don't stop until the whole video is filmed.** The first 1–3 minutes feel stiff and incoherent; accept it. A 60-second video takes him 6–7 minutes of recording. (10:51–11:31)
- "The desire to not mess up is getting in your head." (11:31)

### T4: Minimum viable editing (11:55)
1. **B-roll:** change it **every new sentence**. With no talking, match the B-roll to the beat. Random footage is fine if it loosely fits ("like Subway Surfers… but… more authentic"). (12:12)
2. **SLC captions:** **S**hort (2–3 words per caption, batch-edited), **L**arge (easy to read), **C**lear (white, classic TikTok font, blurred black shadow). (12:43–13:54)
3. **Music:** **trending songs** for familiarity, using the **instrumental** version so lyrics don't compete with your voice. (14:01)

### T5: Community + the 4 phases (14:59)
- "You are the average of the five people you spend the most time with." Being around creators who improve 1% a day changed his life; "you are quite literally one connection away". (15:38; 18:30)
- Passively watching videos ≠ progress. Growth "only happens when you make content a top priority". (16:21–16:40)
- **The 4 phases (Content Academy):**
  | Phase | Name | Goal | Focus |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | **Fries** (learning) | **1,000 views per video, consistently** | Just show up and post; basics, first 7 videos, then scripting, storytelling, hooks |
  | 2 | **Pebbles** (optimisation) | **First 10,000-view video** | Small details: cutting to the millisecond, camera angles, shot types, lighting, editing |
  | 3 | **Gem** (exponential growth) | **First 100K-view video → first 10,000 followers** | Scripting for your persona; find "your unique edge… the it guy for blank" |
  | 4 | **Money** (monetisation) | **First $1,000 from content** | Turning the audience into income |
- "You can't skip steps." Spending 6 months in phases 1–2 is normal. (25:00–25:13)
- The rest of the video (from ~19:00) is a pitch for Content Academy: live calls, coaches, events, leaderboard games.
